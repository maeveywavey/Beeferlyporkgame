# Setting, story and characters

The game's world, peoples, main story, lords and companions. Everything here is held to `docs/canon.md`; where the two disagree, the canon file wins and this file is wrong.

## 1. The setting

### The Quiet Years
Eleven years ago the Avatar died at sea, on board the *Lantern of Agna Qel'a*, a neutral Water Tribe ship hosting secret peace talks between the Fire Lord's envoys and the Earth Queen's regent. A storm took the ship. The Avatar's body was never recovered, and each side believes the other arranged the death.

What happened, which the game never states outright (`docs/canon.md` §9): the Avatar had been poisoned at the regent's welcome dinner days before, which blocked the Avatar State as Azula's lightning once blocked Aang's. In the storm they held the sea flat around the crew's boats until every boat reached the rocks, and were spent, outside the Avatar State, when the recalled rescue never came. The crew lived. Logbook pages I, IV and V carry the only hints. Who poisoned the Avatar is left open.

Somewhere, a child is the new Avatar. The sages of that child's nation have begun quietly testing children, as they always do, and say nothing; the game never says which nation. When the child is sixteen they will be told. Until then there is no bridge between the nations, and the people call this time **the Quiet Years**.

**The conflicts at the start of the game**
| War | Cause |
|---|---|
| **Fire Nation vs Earth Kingdom: the War of the Strait** | The Strait Isles lie between the Fire Islands and the Earth Kingdom coast. Both crowns have claimed them for centuries and neither has ever fully held them; their people are mixed. After volcanic winters ruined three Fire Nation harvests, the Admiralty occupied the Isles "to protect Fire Nation families there". The Earth Kingdom calls it theft. Both are partly right. It is a border war over islands, not a conquest: no Fire Nation army stands on the mainland (`docs/canon.md` §6). |
| **Earth Kingdom vs Kingdom of Omashu: the Secession** | King Feng Tao of Omashu stopped sending grain taxes to Ba Sing Se while Omashu's own people went hungry, and declared Omashu free. Ba Sing Se calls him a traitor; the people of Omashu call him a king. |
| **Fire Nation vs Southern Water Tribe: the Raids** | Southern raiders strike Fire Nation supply ships headed for the Strait Isles. The South calls it defending the seas; the Fire Nation calls it piracy. |
| The **Northern Water Tribe** | Neutral, isolationist and divided over whether to help the South. |
| The **Air Nomads** | Never at war. They shelter refugees from every side and are trusted by everyone, which makes them a target. |

**The sky.** The Great Comet passes once a century and may or may not fall within a playthrough (about one game in three); the Fire Sages announce it. Total solar eclipses that silence firebending are almost unknown in recorded history, and none occurs. Lunar eclipses happen and only hide the full moon's strength (`docs/canon.md` §5).

**Tone guide.** The original series could hold a joke and a funeral in the same scene. We do the same. War is never glamorous and never grimdark: people are hurt, people are kind, and kindness costs something.

## 2. Cultures and class: lore with mechanical consequences

Every cultural note below must change something in play. Each one is followed by its **game hook**.

### Earth Kingdom
* **Ba Sing Se's rings.** The Lower Ring houses refugees and labourers, the Middle Ring merchants and scholars, the Upper Ring nobility. Gates between rings are guarded, and the Censorate polices thought as well as crime.
  **Game hook:** Ring access depends on the player's *standing* (Commoner, Citizen, Gentry, Noble). A Middle Ring pass costs gold or a favour. Upper Ring audiences require Gentry status or a noble's invitation. Lower Ring recruits are cheap and eager; Upper Ring shops sell the best equipment.
* **Bureaucracy and seals.** Petitions, tariffs and permits govern trade.
  **Game hook:** Trade permits for Earth Kingdom markets lower tariffs by 10%.
* **Earthbending as labour.** Most earthbenders are miners, builders and farmers, not soldiers.
  **Game hook:** Village recruits may be earthbenders; the share depends on how prosperous the village is.

### Kingdom of Omashu
* A proud mountain city of delivery chutes, giant stone stairways and loud public theatre. Its king rules with spectacle, and its couriers are folk heroes.
  **Game hook:** Omashu troops are hardier but less disciplined (more HP, less armour). Omashu caravans move faster.

### Fire Nation
* **Noble houses and honour.** Status is inherited, and nobles settle disputes with an **Agni Kai**, a firebending duel witnessed by others.
  **Game hook:** Fire Nation lords may challenge a disrespectful player to an Agni Kai, and the player may challenge them in turn. The duel is a one-on-one battle with renown and honour at stake. A non-bender may name a firebending champion (a companion).
* **Conscription and the Isles.** Commoners are conscripted. Strait Islanders are torn between two crowns.
  **Game hook:** Strait Isles villages are mixed: Fire Nation and Earth Kingdom families. Raiding them angers both nations.
* **Fire Sages** run the temples, keep the calendar and watch for the Great Comet.

### Northern Water Tribe
* **The Law of the Two Paths.** Women may learn waterbending only as healing; men may learn it as combat. It is an old law, and the elders defend it as tradition, not malice. Some women learn combat in secret.
  **Game hook:** Northern women recruits can become only Healers until the law changes. The companion **Nima's** story can change it. If it does, a new Northern troop line opens (*Moon Guard*: women waterbending warriors) and the Tribe's council relations shift.
* **Arranged marriages and the council** tie families together politically.

### Southern Water Tribe
* In this era the South is a great city of ice and an equal of the North: seafaring, fewer laws, and more voices in council. Benders of all genders fight.
  **Game hook:** The South's council votes on war and peace. A player with high Southern standing can address it and sway those votes.

### Air Nomads
* **Pacifism and refuge.** The Nomads will not fight. They heal anyone and shelter refugees. Monks and nuns live in separate temples: the Southern and Northern temples are monks', the Eastern and Western nuns'. They are vegetarian.
  **Game hook:** Air Temples cannot be besieged. Attacking Nomads collapses your honour and every nation's opinion of you. Resting at a temple heals your party for free, and temple markets sell no meat. Pilgrim parties on sky bison roam the map; they can be talked to, but they never fight. Monks might join you as healers and scouts, never as fighters.

### Peoples on the margins
* **Sandbenders** of the Si Wong: clan-based, distrustful of outsiders, masters of reading vibrations through the sand.
* **Foggy Swamp Tribe:** isolated waterbenders tending one great tree.
* **Daofei:** sworn brotherhoods of outlaws with their own codes. The *Red Lanterns* rob tax barges and feed the Lower Ring, which makes them heroes to some and murderers to others.

## 3. The main story: *The Drowned Testament*

### Outline
**Prologue: the courier (day 2–5).** A wounded Air Nomad courier, **Brother Norbu**, reaches the player's starting settlement carrying an oilcloth packet: the first page of the Avatar's logbook, recovered from the wreck by Southern divers. Daofei are hunting him. He asks the player to take the packet to the Eastern Air Temple, and dies, or survives if the player fights off the daofei in time.
> *"I was told to carry it to someone who wanted nothing from it. I couldn't find anyone like that. So I'm giving it to you, and hoping."*

**Act I: the scattered pages.** Five pages were sold, stolen or hidden after the wreck. Each page is a quest with at least two solutions:
| Page | Holder | Ways to get it |
|---|---|---|
| II | **Old Mother Shiyo**, a pirate queen near Full Moon Bay | Defeat her fleet; buy it (1,500 gold); or, with **Reika**, call in an old debt |
| III | **The Eastern Air Temple** archive | Earn the Abbess's trust by escorting refugees; or, with **Pemba**, ask as family |
| IV | A **Censorate vault** in Ba Sing Se | Steal it (the Censorate becomes hostile); petition the Earth Queen (needs Gentry standing); or, with **Hana**, use her old tunnels |
| V | **Admiralty records** at the Fire Navy Harbour | Storm the harbour; bribe a clerk; or, with **Juro**, who knows the filing system he used to curse |
| VI | **Wan Shi Tong's Library** | The owl spirit demands knowledge of equal worth. You may give a companion's secret (they will know), a secret of your own (lose experience), or the Air Temple refugee route (refugees die later). There is no free option. |

**Act II: the truth.** With all six pages the logbook reveals what happened. The Avatar's final entry, written in the storm:
> *"The rudder is gone and the boats have turned back. I don't know whose hands did either. I know whose fault it will be called. Whoever finds this — I'm sorry I wasn't enough. I don't want my death to be a reason. Please let it be an ending."*

**Act III: the choice.** The player decides what to do with the truth:
| Ending | Requirement | World consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **The Truth Told** | Convene a Council of Nations at Hamakaze (renown 300, peace with at least three nations) and read it aloud | The Fire Nation and Earth Kingdom sign the Hamakaze Accord. Kuro and Wu are disgraced, flee and raise rebel fleets for the final battles. Every nation's opinion of the player rises. |
| **The Truth Sold** | Give it to one ruler | That nation gains a just cause against the other; the war intensifies with the player on the winning side. Rewards include a fief and title. |
| **The Truth Buried** | Burn it or sell it to Kuro or Wu | The war continues and the player is paid. The epilogue is quietly bleak. |
| **The Truth Kept** | Give it to the Air Nomads for the new Avatar, to be read at sixteen | No world change now. The epilogue shows a sixteen-year-old reading the last page aloud. |

The Omashu Secession is resolved separately, through **Shen Mu's** letter.

## 5. Companions

Representation brief, agreed by both teams: queer and neurodivergent players are a large part of this audience. Represent them as **people whose identities are part of them, never the plot problem to be solved**. No one is punished for who they are. The neurodivergent characters' traits are written from lived-experience notes and show strengths, costs and accommodations, not quirks. No bury-your-gays deaths: companions can die only through the player's tactical or moral choices, which apply equally to all.

### 5.1 Hana of the Lower Ring (Earth Kingdom, earthbender)

* **Want:** to finish the map. She has charted every tunnel, cellar and fault line under Ba Sing Se, and the map is how she makes a city that overwhelms her make sense.
* **Wound:** her mentor, Grandmother Zhu, was sealed inside a collapsing tunnel by the Censorate during a raid. Hana heard the stones settle, and has not dug in that district since.
* **Voice:** precise and literal. Blunt without meaning harm, and funny without trying.
  > "You said the bridge was 'pretty safe'. Bridges are either load-rated or not. This one is not."
* **In play:** in crowded towns she sometimes asks to wait outside (a dialogue toast; no penalty). In tunnels, caves and sieges she gives a large bonus: she reads ground vibrations and reveals hidden passages in the walkable settlements. She hates surprises; telling her plans ahead raises her approval.
* **Romance:** available to women and nonbinary player characters. She courts by making you a map.

Her personal quest is **"The Settled Stones"**: the Censorate officer who sealed the tunnel, now Chief Censor Shu Fan's lieutenant **Bo Lai**, can be exposed to the Queen, killed in the tunnels, or spared. Sparing him gives Hana a scene in which she chooses not to become the thing that hurt her, unlocks her final earthbending technique and makes the Dai Li testify in Act III. Killing him fulfils her vow, but she withdraws for a week and loses her bonus. Exposing him needs Gentry standing and weakens Wu in Act III.

### 5.2 Juro Sato (Fire Nation, firebender who cannot bend)

* **Want:** to make his hands do what they used to. He was an artillery engineer who designed catapult fire-pots, and he lost his fire the day one of his designs burned a village in the Strait Isles. He deserted that night.
* **Identity:** Juro is a trans man. His family accepted him; his father wrote him letters every week of the war. The loss of his fire is guilt, not identity. The game never questions his manhood, and neither do its characters.
* **Voice:** dry, self-deprecating, deeply kind.
  > "My father writes that the plum tree finally fruited. That's the whole letter. It's the best letter I've ever read."
* **In play:** an engineer. He repairs siege ladders twice as fast (siege preparation halved) and can build fire-pots that work for non-benders. He has no bending until his quest ends.

**Personal quest: "Breath Before Flame".** Visit the burned village, now rebuilding. A survivor, **Mi-sun**, will not forgive him. He does not ask her to. The player can help rebuild (gold, labour and three days) or leave. At the Fire Sages' temple Juro attempts the breath forms. If the village was helped and Mi-sun spoke to him, even coldly, his fire returns, small and blue-white at its heart. He lights her lantern at the village festival and she says, "It's a good lantern." That is all. **Team Lantern's note: this scene is where players should cry. Do not add music.**

### 5.3 Pemba (Air Nomad monk, nonbinary, they/them)

* **Voice:** gentle, deadpan, brutal at pai sho.
  > "Violence is always a choice. So is lunch. I've chosen lunch."
* **In play:** will not deal killing blows; their attacks knock enemies out instead, so more enemies become prisoners. Heals party wounds overnight. Their approval drops sharply when the player executes prisoners or raids villages.

**Personal quest: "The Lantern and the Wind".** Dawa robs a tax barge to feed the Lower Ring. The choices:
* Turn her in: Pemba leaves the party, and returns only in the Truth Kept ending.
* Bring her home to the temple: she resents it, but lives.
* Let her choose: she stays with the Red Lanterns, who become friendly to the player and, if Hana's tunnels are known, feed the Lower Ring.

Each outcome changes the Lower Ring's prosperity and the daofei's attitude.

### 5.4 Nima (Northern Water Tribe healer, secretly a combat waterbender)
**Design note:** Her arc must make the Law of the Two Paths a real choice for the Tribe, not a strawman. Master Aklaq must have a good argument.
Aklaq's argument: the law was written after a famine war in which the Tribe nearly lost every healer. "When everyone fights, no one mends." Nima's answer: "Then let the ones who want to mend, mend, and the ones who want to fight, fight. Kaya wants both."
* **Personal quest: "The Two Paths".** Return north. Nima can duel Aklaq (a waterbending fight the player watches and can influence by preparing her), petition the council with Elder Pana (needs Northern standing), or decide to raise Kaya in the South instead.
  * Win the duel or the petition: the law changes. *Moon Guard* troops unlock, and Aklaq resigns, then later teaches Kaya himself, a small grace.
  * Leaving: Nima is free, the North is unchanged, and Northern relations drop.
* **Voice:** warm, precise, impatient with nonsense. Her humour comes from parenting.
  > "I have negotiated with a seven-year-old about boots. Your war council doesn't frighten me."

### 5.5 Ukiuk (Southern Water Tribe harpooner, ADHD, bisexual)
**Design note:** The guilt must be specific. Which raid, and whose face?
In Qannik's raid on the Fire Nation island of Shu Jing, Ukiuk's harpoon line snagged a fishing boat that was not a warship; a fisherman, **Haru-ko's father**, drowned. Ukiuk sends money to the widow anonymously every season. He has never been to see her.
* **In play:** gives boats and sea travel +30% speed. He is restless in long sieges (approval drops) and a brilliant improviser: in battle he sometimes grants a free tactical action.
* **Personal quest: "Debts at Sea".** Visit Shu Jing. The widow, **Kimi**, can learn who he is or not. If he confesses, she hits him, then feeds him: "You'll eat. You look like a harpoon." He stops raiding, and Qannik calls him a traitor. If he hides it, nothing changes and his money keeps arriving. Each choice changes Southern–Fire relations by a little.
* **Romance:** available to any player character; he flirts by building you things.

### 5.6 Shen Mu (Omashu chute-courier, 19, stutters, dyslexic, gay)
**Design note:** Comedy is welcome, but his disability must not be the joke. The joke is what he says, never how he says it.
Shen Mu stutters on hard consonants and reads slowly. He memorises every route instead, and can recite three hundred addresses. He carries **the letter**: Princess Ryoko's secret peace proposal to Consort Jiang Wei, which both sides would kill to intercept.
* **Text presentation:** render his stutter sparingly and consistently, as repeated first syllables ("I d-don't…"), never more than once per line, and give players a toggle to show his lines without it if they find it hard to read.
* **Personal quest: "Signed, Sealed".** Deliver the letter through the war, picking a route: the chutes, the canyon or the sea. Ambushes depend on the route. Delivery opens Omashu–Fire Nation talks, and possibly Omashu–Earth Kingdom peace if Queen Hualing is involved. If the player reads the letter, Shen Mu's approval plummets ("A letter is a promise. You don't open someone's promise"), but the player gains leverage.
* **His own story:** he is in love with Captain Doru's son, the courier **Lin**, and is too shy to say so. The player can help him. At the end of the war, if Omashu stands, they open a courier post together.

### 5.7 Captain Reika Kuroda (Fire Nation privateer, sapphic)
**Design note:** Not gold; brass. Make her morally grey. She sold the logbook's Page II to Old Mother Shiyo before she knew what it was.
Reika sails under a Fire Nation letter of marque, raiding Earth Kingdom shipping for a cut, and is beginning to suspect that her employer, Admiral Kuro, is lying about everything. Her ex-partner, the pirate **Shiyo's daughter Rin**, is on the other side.
* **In play:** a ranged duellist with naval bonuses. She opens smuggling routes (better prices in ports).
* **Personal quest: "Letters of Marque".** Will she burn her commission? If she exposes Kuro's cargo ledgers, Act III's case against Kuro is stronger. If she keeps sailing for him, she becomes wealthy and bitter, and may betray the player in Act III if her approval is low.
* **Voice:**
  > "I'm not a hero. I'm a woman with a ship and a very flexible definition of 'cargo'."

### 5.8 Kesi (Sandbender tracker, Deaf)
**Design note:** Sandbender Deaf culture should be a culture, not a superpower. How do sandbenders communicate in sandstorms? A sign language their whole clan uses would follow naturally.
The Si Wong clans share **storm-sign**, a hand language developed because nobody can hear anything inside a sandstorm. Kesi signs. Hearing characters who travel with her learn it; the party gains the *Storm-Sign* perk, and battle orders are never lost in chaos (morale bonus). She reads lips and vibrations, and she was exiled from her clan for refusing to join a raid on a merchant caravan carrying children.
* **Text presentation:** Kesi's dialogue appears in a distinct signed-speech style (italic, with hand-shape notes in the accessible mode). Other characters sign back once they learn.
* **Personal quest: "The Quiet Sand".** Her clan is starving because the Earth Kingdom closed the oasis wells. The player can negotiate well access with Misty Palms (a trade quest), fight the garrison, or help the clan move to the Foggy Swamp, the strangest and best option, which requires Pemba or Nima to mediate.

## 6. Sample banter (camp conversations)

**Hana & Ukiuk**
> **Ukiuk:** I made you a rock shelf! For your rocks!
> **Hana:** These are survey samples.
> **Ukiuk:** For your survey samples!
> **Hana:** …It is level. You used a level.
> **Ukiuk:** I used my *eyes*.
> **Hana:** It is level by accident. I'm keeping it.

**Juro & Reika**
> **Reika:** You were in the artillery corps. You must have hated sailors.
> **Juro:** I designed things that set ships on fire. It wasn't personal.
> **Reika:** It's always personal when it's your ship.
> **Juro:** …Yes. I'm learning that.

**Pemba & Kesi** (signed)
> **Pemba:** *(signing slowly)* I have learned "food", "bison" and "where".
> **Kesi:** *(signing)* That's most of life.
> **Pemba:** *(signing)* "Where food?" "Bison where?"
> **Kesi:** *(laughing, signing)* You're fluent.

**Nima & Shen Mu**
> **Shen Mu:** D-do you think he likes me? Lin. Not — hypothetically.
> **Nima:** I think a boy who walks four miles out of his route to deliver your mail personally is not doing it for the exercise.
> **Shen Mu:** …Oh no.
> **Nima:** Oh yes.
