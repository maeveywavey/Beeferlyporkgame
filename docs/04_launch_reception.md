# Launch Reception (Simulation)

> **Simulated.** This is a forecast of how the game would plausibly be received, written by Claude from comparable releases and the build's real strengths and weaknesses. No outlet, reviewer, streamer or sales figure below is real. Outlet names are invented.

## 0. The precondition nobody can skip

*Avatar: The Last Airbender* is owned by Paramount (Nickelodeon), and this game uses its world, peoples and bending. **As built, it cannot legally be sold.** It could be released free as a fan project and risk a takedown, or sold under a licence.

This simulation assumes the second path. A mid-size publisher licenses the setting from Paramount Global's games division and ships the game on Steam. The finished game matches this repository's design, built out by the 14-person team implied in `docs/01_team_review.md` over about two years. The licence costs a minimum guarantee plus about 12% royalty.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Title** | *Avatar: The Quiet Years* (working title "Mount & Bend") |
| **Platforms** | PC (Steam). Console ports announced, not shipped at launch. |
| **Price** | US$29.99, Early Access for 9 months, then 1.0 |
| **Development cost** | about US$3.6M, including the licence minimum guarantee |
| **Team** | 14 core staff plus contractors |

## 1. Critics (1.0 launch)

Simulated aggregate: **74 / 100** from 41 critic reviews (range 55–90).

| Outlet (fictional) | Score | Pull quote |
|---|---|---|
| *Long Read Games* | 90 | "The best writing in a licensed game in years. Juro's lantern scene will be in 'best of the year' lists." |
| *Sandbox Monthly* | 82 | "Warband's bones with a living politics on top. Omashu's Secession is the most interesting war I've fought in a strategy game." |
| *The Polygon Herald* | 78 | "Queer and neurodivergent characters written by people who clearly listened. Nobody is a lesson; everybody is a person." |
| *PC Frontline* | 70 | "Combat is serviceable, bending is pretty, and nobody will mistake it for the show's choreography." |
| *Pixel & Ink* | 66 | "A gorgeous ink-line style let down by asset-pack faces and creatures. The ostrich horse is a horse. The sky bison is, regrettably, a cow." |
| *Console Critic* | 55 | "Menus wearing a beautiful hat. Too much of the world lives in text windows." |

**Consensus praise**
- The companions and their quests.
- Choices with world consequences: the Moon Guard unlock, the Accord, Omashu.
- Culture and class systems that make Ba Sing Se, the Fire Nation and the Water Tribes feel distinct.
- Walkable settlements.
- Accessibility options.
- The decision to tell an original story rather than replay the show.

**Consensus criticism**
- Animation and faces read as stock. The bending looks "game-y", not like the show's martial arts.
- The creatures are substitutes.
- The late-game sandbox empties out after the Drowned Testament ends.
- There are no naval battles in a story about admirals.
- Interiors are menus.
- Performance dips in 200-unit battles on older GPUs.

## 2. Players

### 2.1 Steam reviews

| Window | Reviews | Positive | Steam label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early Access, month 1 | 6,800 | 76% | Mostly Positive |
| 1.0 launch week | 4,100 | 84% | Very Positive (recent) |
| 12 months (all time) | 19,500 | 81% | Very Positive |

**Most-upvoted positive themes:**
1. "I came for bending, I stayed for Hana and Kesi."
2. Choices that stick: "I sold the logbook to Kuro and my whole party left me. Correct. I deserved that."
3. M&B veterans enjoying an "actual reason to besiege Omashu."
4. Accessibility: plain speech, text scaling, and the fact that "nothing depends on hearing."
5. "Pronouns are just pronouns" — no character makes the player's identity a plot point.

**Most-upvoted negative themes:**
1. "Why is the bison a cow." This became the community's running joke and the most-shared screenshot of launch week.
2. Bending feels like spellcasting with a stance, not kung fu.
3. Mid-game repetition: bandit-clearing and village quests after day 150.
4. AI lords who march past besieged allies.
5. Faces in close-up portraits.

**Backlash.** A small coordinated wave of negative reviews in launch week (about 900) targeted the queer characters. Steam's off-topic review filter flagged most of them. They moved the all-time score by under 2 points and generated more supportive reviews than they cost. The team did not respond in-game and patched nothing about it, which the community read as the right call.

### 2.2 Community, streaming, mods

**Streaming**
- Twitch peaked at 21k concurrent viewers in 1.0 launch week.
- One variety streamer's six-hour "Pacifist Pemba run" (no killing blows) clipped well.
- The Agni Kai against Commander Daisuke, where the streamer lost and was mocked by chat, went viral.

**Fan work**
- The most popular fan art is of Hana with her map, Kesi and Pemba signing, and Juro's lantern.
- Hana/Kesi became the most-shipped non-canon pair.

**Mods.** The Steam Workshop launched at 1.0 and held 1,400 mods at 12 months. Most-subscribed:
1. "Proper Sky Bison" (a community-made model).
2. "Naval Skirmishes" (a crude but loved ship-boarding mod).
3. "Bigger Ba Sing Se."
4. "More Banter."

**Sales.** At peak, 61k players were active at once (Early Access launch day) and 38k at 1.0. Twelve-month retention is healthy for the genre: 9% monthly active against lifetime owners.

## 3. Sales and money (12 months)

| | Units | Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Early Access (9 months, $29.99, two 20%-off sales) | 210,000 | $5.6M gross |
| 1.0 and first anniversary sale | 165,000 | $4.1M gross |
| **Total** | **375,000** | **$9.7M gross** |

| Deductions | |
|---|---|
| Platform fee (30%; the 25% tier starts above $10M) | -$2.9M |
| Regional pricing, refunds (6%), VAT | -$1.2M |
| Licence royalty (12% of net) | -$0.7M |
| **Net to publisher and developer** | **about $4.9M** |

**Verdict: a modest commercial success and a clear critical-cult success.** It earned out its US$3.6M budget in about eleven months, shortly after 1.0, and funded a sequel greenlight. It was not a breakout. Comparable licensed games of its size usually lose money, and this one didn't, because the writing found an audience the licence alone wouldn't have. That audience was queer and neurodivergent fans of the original series who had waited years for a game that took them seriously.

## 4. What players loved (keep)

1. Companions with wants, fears and specific wounds; quests that change the world.
2. An original story in a loved setting, with no retread of canon events.
3. Culture and class as systems, not set dressing.
4. Choices that cost something and stay costly.
5. Accessibility treated as a default, not a patch.
6. The cel and ink look of the environments.

## 5. What players disliked (fix in the sequel)

1. Faces, bodies and creatures that read as stock assets.
2. Bending that does not look like the martial arts the show is built on.
3. Interiors as menus, and a Ba Sing Se without walkable rings.
4. No naval or aerial play.
5. Thin endgame after the main story; a lot of repetition by day 150.
6. Lord AI that doesn't coordinate.
7. Every bender in an element casting the same three moves. "Why can't I have *my* firebending?"

The seventh complaint ended up the most actionable, and it is the seed of the sequel's headline feature: see `docs/05_sequel_design.md`.
